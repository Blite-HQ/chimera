# Notes — Class 10

## Resumen

Ninth session. Opens by generalizing QUBO to handle higher-than-quadratic terms via an auxiliary-variable reduction trick (motivated by hardware's two-local coupling constraint), applies it to derive a full Max-3SAT QUBO formulation. The remainder of the session builds the physics background for variational quantum algorithms: eigenvalues/eigenvectors, the Hamiltonian as a Hermitian operator, unitary time evolution U=e^{-iHt} and its link to the Schrödinger equation, measurement in arbitrary bases, expected values vs. probabilities, the variational principle (E≥E₀ bound enabling VQE), and finally the Ising model — its 2D-lattice cost Hamiltonian, its NP-hardness, and the explicit algebraic mapping showing QUBO and Ising formulations are equivalent (via xᵢ=(1−sᵢ)/2), illustrated on MaxCut. Session explicitly framed as shorter, since more time is expected in the accompanying notebooks; announces tomorrow's topic as quantum annealing/adiabatic algorithms.

## Conceptos clave

- **Recap and caveat on formulations** [~00:00]: yesterday's QUBO formulation of TSP is explicitly one possible formulation among many (other QUBO variants and non-QUBO formulations of TSP exist); reiterated that the QUBO formulation itself contains nothing inherently quantum — a classical optimizer could solve the same QUBO.
- **Motivation for restricting to quadratic terms** [~00:00]: QUBO's quadratic-only restriction is not arbitrary — it's motivated by the underlying quantum hardware being "two-local" (only pairwise qubit couplings are physically realizable at a given time), so higher-order (cubic+) terms in an objective function must be reduced to quadratic before mapping to hardware.
- **Degree-reduction technique (auxiliary variable substitution)** [~00:00]: to handle a higher-degree term like xᵢxⱼxₖ, pick a pair of variables in the highest-degree term (e.g., xᵢ,xⱼ), define a new binary variable yᵢⱼ intended to represent xᵢxⱼ, and substitute yᵢⱼ everywhere that product appears — reducing the term's degree by one. Since the optimizer doesn't inherently know yᵢⱼ=xᵢxⱼ, a penalty term must enforce this relationship.
- **Penalty for enforcing yᵢⱼ=xᵢxⱼ** [~00:00]: given penalty expression P·(xᵢxⱼ − 2xᵢyᵢⱼ − 2xⱼyᵢⱼ + 3yᵢⱼ), verified via truth table to equal 0 whenever yᵢⱼ=xᵢxⱼ holds, and >0 (positive) in all 4 cases where yᵢⱼ differs from xᵢxⱼ — confirmed by direct substitution (instructor works through the mismatch cases live, leaves full table-filling as an exercise, notes the worked table is posted separately in the slides/notes).
- **3SAT and Max-3SAT** [~00:00–20:00]: a Boolean formula φ in conjunctive normal form (CNF) = AND of n clauses, each clause = OR of up to 3 literals (a variable or its negation) — this restricted form is called 3SAT; any Boolean formula can be converted to this form. 3SAT (decision): does there exist a variable assignment making φ evaluate to 1 (satisfiable)? Max-3SAT (optimization): maximize the number of clauses satisfied (not requiring all n to be satisfied) — the natural optimization relaxation of the decision problem, mirroring the MaxCut decision/optimization split from c08–c09.
- **Building gᵢ(x) for a single clause** [~00:00–20:00]: for a 3-literal clause (xᵢ∨xⱼ∨xₖ, no negations case worked through explicitly), the expression g = xᵢ+xⱼ+xₖ−xᵢxⱼ−xᵢxₖ−xⱼxₖ+xᵢxⱼxₖ is verified via full truth table (8 rows) to equal 1 whenever at least one literal is true and 0 when all are false — i.e. it correctly implements logical OR arithmetically. Three further cases (exactly one, two, or all three literals negated) follow analogous constructions with sign flips, left mostly as exercises (only the general pattern and the "all negated" flip behavior — where the expression is 0 only when all three variables are 1 — are called out explicitly).
- **Max-3SAT objective**: maximize F(x) = Σᵢ gᵢ(x) over all clauses i=1..n, using the appropriate gᵢ per clause's negation pattern; converting the resulting expression's cubic term (xᵢxⱼxₖ) into QUBO form requires applying the degree-reduction/penalty technique from earlier in the session — described as mechanical/automatable but worth seeing worked through once by hand. [~20:00]
- **Variational quantum algorithms (VQAs)** [~20:00]: a broad NISQ-era algorithm class built on a parameterized quantum circuit; the circuit's output state depends on tunable parameters, a cost is computed from measurement of that output, a classical optimizer updates the parameters to improve the cost, and the loop repeats (hybrid quantum-classical loop). Explicitly noted as hard to prove general complexity-theoretic results about (unlike Grover's algorithm), though special cases may have results; applications span quantum chemistry, combinatorial optimization, and quantum machine learning.
- **Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)** [~20:00]: a VQA subclass aimed at finding a quantum system's ground state (lowest-energy configuration) — directly relevant to optimization since minimizing an objective can be mapped to minimizing a Hamiltonian's expected energy.
- **Eigenvalue/eigenvector (linear-algebra prerequisite, reviewed)** [~20:00]: for matrix A and vector v_λ, if Av_λ=λv_λ then v_λ is an eigenvector and λ its eigenvalue — flagged as a QBronze prerequisite topic, briefly re-reviewed here.
- **Hamiltonian and Hermitian operators** [~20:00]: the ground state corresponds to the lowest eigenvalue of the system's Hamiltonian H. A Hamiltonian is a Hermitian operator, defined by H=H† (dagger = entrywise complex conjugate + transpose). Hermitian operators represent the total energy of a quantum system; their eigenvalues correspond to physically measurable/real outcomes.
- **Unitary evolution and its relation to the Hamiltonian** [~20:00]: U = e^{−iHt} relates a state at time 0 to its evolution at time t (continuous-time picture); this connects to the Schrödinger equation (H|ψ⟩ appears in the differential equation solved to obtain this exponential form). Explicitly contrasted with the earlier discrete-step/gate-based circuit picture used throughout the course — this is the continuous-time physics picture underlying it. Student question about the connection to Euler's formula acknowledged as a special case of this more general matrix-exponential expression.
- **Hermitian operators as observables and measurement in arbitrary bases** [~20:00–40:00]: unlike the computational (Z) basis default used so far, measurement can occur in any basis, and Hermitian operators are the natural language for arbitrary "observables." Key property: Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues (unlike general unitary matrices), matching the requirement that measurement outcomes are real numbers.
- **Spectral decomposition and projective measurement** [~40:00]: any Hermitian H can be written as H=ΣλλP_λ, where P_λ=|ψ_λ⟩⟨ψ_λ| projects onto the eigenspace of eigenvalue λ. Probability of outcome λ = ⟨ψ|P_λ|ψ⟩ = |⟨ψ_λ|ψ⟩|² — recognized as the generalized Born rule / standard projective-measurement formula (absolute-value signs needed since amplitudes may now be complex, unlike the real-amplitude treatment in QBronze).
- **Expected value of an observable** [~40:00]: E(H) under state ψ = Σλ·λ·P(λ) = ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ — derived directly from the spectral decomposition and probability formula. Key conceptual distinction drawn: ⟨ψ|M|ψ⟩ evaluates a *probability* when M is a projector, but evaluates an *expected value* when M is a full Hermitian operator (same "sandwich" notation, different meaning depending on what's inside).
- **Measuring in the X basis via Z-basis measurement** [~40:00]: expected value in X basis relates to Z basis via Z=HXH (H=Hadamard here, not Hamiltonian — potential notational clash flagged); equivalently, apply Hadamard to |ψ⟩ first (getting |ψ'⟩=H|ψ⟩), then measure normally in the Z basis. Y-basis measurement follows an analogous (more algebra-heavy) identity, not spelled out.
- **Variational principle** [~40:00]: for any state |ψ⟩ (not necessarily an eigenstate), the expected energy E=⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ is always ≥ the ground state energy E₀ — intuitively true since E₀ is by definition the lowest possible energy. This gives a valid upper bound on E₀ from any candidate state, which is exactly what a VQE optimization loop exploits: prepare a parameterized state, measure ⟨ψ(θ)|H|ψ(θ)⟩, use a classical optimizer to update θ toward lower expected energy, iterate. Proof deferred to the posted slides/notes (not included in the transcript itself).
- **VQE loop structure (informal)** [~40:00]: parameterized circuit → measure/estimate expected value ⟨H⟩ → classical optimizer proposes new parameters θ_{k+1} → re-run circuit with updated parameters → repeat. Explicitly noted this general VQA/VQE framework is *not* directly used in the QCobalt notebooks but is presented as conceptual grounding before tomorrow's adiabatic/annealing algorithms.
- **The Ising model** [~40:00–60:00]: a physical system of a lattice of qubits (1D historically, but the course uses a 2D lattice/grid) with pairwise (two-neighbor) interactions plus optional external field terms — directly analogous to QUBO's quadratic-term restriction. Each qubit/spin can be in state 0 or 1 (or equivalently spin up/down); a "configuration" specifies every qubit's state; the lowest-energy configuration is the ground state, and finding it is a minimization problem. Explicitly noted: this Ising ground-state problem is itself NP-hard to solve classically, so mapping other NP-hard/NP-complete problems onto it is a natural — if not yet practically scalable — target for quantum algorithms.
- **Ising cost Hamiltonian** [~60:00]: H_C = Σᵢ hᵢZᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼ ZᵢZⱼ (external field terms hᵢ plus pairwise coupling terms Jᵢⱼ, using Pauli-Z operators in place of classical spin variables). Applying H_C to a lattice eigenstate |ψ⟩ (product of individual Z-eigenstates with eigenvalues sᵢ=±1) yields the expected energy expression Σᵢ hᵢsᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼsᵢsⱼ — matching the classical Ising energy formula directly.
- **Ising ↔ QUBO mapping for MaxCut** [~60:00]: switch from {0,1} (QUBO xᵢ) to {+1,−1} (Ising sᵢ, spin variable), with sᵢ=1 if vertex i∈S, −1 if in R. Edge term sᵢsⱼ acts like a "product-based" parity check (=1 same group, =−1 different groups, i.e. crossing edge) analogous to QUBO's xᵢ⊕xⱼ-style parity check but multiplicative instead of additive. Cut size = ½Σ_{(i,j)∈E}(1−sᵢsⱼ) (the (1−sᵢsⱼ) term is 0 for same-group pairs and 2 for crossing pairs, hence the ½ normalization) — converted to a minimization problem as minimize ½Σ(sᵢsⱼ−1), and since the constant −1 term can be dropped in a minimization, the Ising cost Hamiltonian reduces to simply ΣZᵢZⱼ for MaxCut, matching the general Ising form.
- **Explicit QUBO↔Ising equivalence derivation** [~60:00]: using the substitution xᵢ=(1−sᵢ)/2 (converting QUBO's {0,1} variable into Ising's {+1,−1} spin), the instructor plugs this into the known MaxCut QUBO objective (−xᵢ−xⱼ+2xᵢxⱼ) and, after algebraic simplification (the linear sᵢ,sⱼ terms cancel, constants combine), arrives exactly at ½(sᵢsⱼ−1) — confirming the two formulations are mathematically equivalent, not just similar in spirit. Live audience question clarifies that the choice of substitution sign (xᵢ=(1±sᵢ)/2) determines whether the resulting problem is framed as a max or min, tying back to which formulation (Kubo max vs. Ising min) is being targeted.
- **Practical implementation note** [~60:00]: the D-Wave Ocean SDK implements these QUBO↔Ising conversions directly via its "binary quadratic model" (BQM) abstraction — described as not a new model per se, just Ocean's unified representation/interconversion layer for QUBO and Ising formulations, with dedicated notebooks covering both the Ising model itself and its application to MaxCut/TSP.
- **Session close** [~60:00]: session intentionally kept shorter since more time is expected in the accompanying notebooks; explicit reassurance that the quantum-physics background (Hamiltonians, Hermitian operators, spectral decomposition, etc.) is helpful context but not required for completing the assessments, which focus on the D-Wave Ocean SDK programming workflow rather than the underlying physics; some later notebooks optionally allow running on actual D-Wave hardware (not required, credits not provided). ## Fórmulas y definiciones

- **Degree-reduction penalty**: for yᵢⱼ≡xᵢxⱼ, penalty = P·(xᵢxⱼ − 2xᵢyᵢⱼ − 2xⱼyᵢⱼ + 3yᵢⱼ), =0 iff yᵢⱼ=xᵢxⱼ, >0 otherwise. [~00:00]
- **3SAT clause satisfiability (no negations)**: g(xᵢ,xⱼ,xₖ) = xᵢ+xⱼ+xₖ−xᵢxⱼ−xᵢxₖ−xⱼxₖ+xᵢxⱼxₖ (=1 iff at least one literal true). [~00:00–20:00]
- **Max-3SAT objective**: maximize F(x) = Σᵢ gᵢ(x), i=1..n clauses. [~20:00]
- **Eigenvalue equation**: Av_λ = λv_λ. [~20:00]
- **Hermitian condition**: H = H† (H dagger = conjugate transpose). [~20:00]
- **Unitary evolution from Hamiltonian**: U = e^{−iHt}, applied as U|ψ(0)⟩ = |ψ(t)⟩; relates to Schrödinger's equation. [~20:00]
- **Spectral decomposition**: H = Σλ λ·P_λ, P_λ=|ψ_λ⟩⟨ψ_λ|. [~40:00]
- **Projective measurement probability**: P(λ) = ⟨ψ|P_λ|ψ⟩ = |⟨ψ_λ|ψ⟩|². [~40:00]
- **Expected value of observable H**: E(H) = ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ = Σλ λ·P(λ). [~40:00]
- **X-basis measurement via Z-basis**: Z=HXH (H=Hadamard); measure ⟨ψ'|X|ψ'⟩ in Z basis where |ψ'⟩=H|ψ⟩. [~40:00]
- **Variational principle**: ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ ≥ E₀ (ground-state energy) for any |ψ⟩. [~40:00]
- **Ising cost Hamiltonian**: H_C = Σᵢ hᵢZᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼ ZᵢZⱼ; expected energy = Σᵢ hᵢsᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼsᵢsⱼ, sᵢ=±1. [~40:00–60:00]
- **MaxCut in Ising spin variables**: sᵢ=+1 (vertex∈S) or −1 (vertex∈R); cut size = ½Σ_{(i,j)∈E}(1−sᵢsⱼ). [~60:00]
- **MaxCut Ising minimization**: minimize ½Σ(sᵢsⱼ−1) ≡ minimize Σ ZᵢZⱼ (dropping the constant). [~60:00]
- **QUBO↔Ising variable mapping**: xᵢ = (1−sᵢ)/2. [~60:00]

## Glosario

- **Auxiliary/substitution variable (yᵢⱼ)**: a new binary variable introduced to stand in for a product xᵢxⱼ, reducing a higher-degree QUBO term's degree, enforced via a penalty term. [~00:00]
- **Two-local (hardware constraint)**: the physical restriction that quantum hardware naturally realizes only pairwise (2-qubit) couplings, motivating QUBO's quadratic-only structure. [~00:00]
- **CNF (conjunctive normal form)**: a Boolean formula expressed as an AND of clauses, each clause an OR of literals. [~00:00–20:00]
- **3SAT / Max-3SAT**: the decision (satisfiability) and optimization (max clauses satisfied) versions of a CNF formula restricted to ≤3 literals per clause. [~00:00–20:00]
- **Variational quantum algorithm (VQA)**: a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm class using a parameterized circuit and classical optimization loop. [~20:00]
- **Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)**: a VQA aimed at estimating a Hamiltonian's ground-state (lowest) eigenvalue. [~20:00]
- **Hamiltonian (H)**: a Hermitian operator representing a quantum system's total energy; its lowest eigenvalue is the ground-state energy. [~20:00]
- **Hermitian operator**: a matrix equal to its own conjugate transpose (H=H†); has real eigenvalues, used to represent observables. [~20:00]
- **Ground state / ground state energy**: the lowest-energy configuration/eigenvalue of a quantum system's Hamiltonian. [~20:00]
- **Spectral decomposition, projector (P_λ)**: expressing a Hermitian operator as a weighted sum of projectors onto its eigenspaces. [~40:00]
- **Observable**: a Hermitian operator whose eigenvalues correspond to possible real-valued measurement outcomes; its expected value is ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩. [~40:00]
- **Variational principle**: the inequality ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩≥E₀, underlying why VQE's iterative minimization converges toward (an upper bound approaching) the true ground state. [~40:00]
- **Ising model**: a lattice-of-spins physical model with pairwise couplings and optional external field, whose ground-state problem is NP-hard and structurally analogous to QUBO. [~40:00–60:00]
- **Spin variable (sᵢ)**: Ising's ±1-valued variable, related to QUBO's {0,1} variable via xᵢ=(1−sᵢ)/2. [~60:00]
- **Binary Quadratic Model (BQM)**: D-Wave Ocean SDK's unified abstraction/interconversion layer for QUBO and Ising formulations (not a new mathematical model). [~60:00]
