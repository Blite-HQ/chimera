# Notes — Course materials (slides/docs), file 5

## Resumen

Second and final session of "module two" pre-hackathon training, led by Alex Ben-Nasconi (OQI, project management / impact side of use-case development), moderated by Audrey, following up on Francesca's technical session (s04). Covers what "multilateral" means and grounds OQI's own multilateral-governance identity via analogy to the G7, WTO, ICRC, and CERN; a history of the UN (League of Nations → UN founding 1945 → post-Cold-War strengthening); the UN's Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) as the successor to the Millennium Development Goals, the "five P's" (people, planet, prosperity, peace, partnerships), and OQI's mission to accelerate SDG progress via quantum computing; OQI's four-part use-case approach (multi-stakeholder collaboration, phased development, community building, impact); a live quiz; and the core technical content — the "theory of change" impact-design methodology, illustrated first via a worked fruit-production/land-allocation use case, then via a guided group exercise revisiting the water-leak-detection use case (previously introduced technically by Francesca in s04) from an impact-mapping perspective.

## Conceptos clave

- **Speaker and session framing** [~00:00]: Alex Ben-Nasconi, OQI, working alongside Francesca on use-case development — Francesca covers the technical side, Alex covers project management and (the focus of this session) the impact side; this is the second/final session of "module two," following Francesca's technical session ("yesterday," i.e. s04) which already covered OQI's A1 use-case package and hackathon challenges.
- **Defining "multilateral"** [~00:00]: bilateral = cooperation between two parties; multilateral = cooperation among three or more countries on agreed/shared goals with common ground rules, based on dialogue and international institutions — explained because OQI's own website description ("a multilateral governance initiative...") uses this term without defining it.
- **Worked examples of multilateralism** [~00:00]: G7 (major economies discussing shared economic problems); World Trade Organization (common cross-border trade rules); ICRC/International Committee of the Red Cross (neutral platform for humanitarian dialogue, e.g. prisoner exchanges in conflict); CERN (multi-member-state particle-physics research organization, and OQI's host institution) — used to establish that multilateralism addresses global problems (climate change, pandemics, peace/security, diplomacy, economic and scientific cooperation) no single country can solve alone.
- **History of the United Nations** [~00:00–20:00]: predecessor was the League of Nations (1920–1946), intended to maintain peace/security but unable to prevent World War II; the UN was founded 24 October 1945 with 51 initial member states, initially weakened during the Cold War by East-West blocs; strengthened after the Cold War (from the 1990s on) into a more active peacekeeping/human-rights/humanitarian-aid organization; now has 193 member states (nearly all countries).
- **UN's purpose and the SDGs' origin** [~20:00]: the UN exists to prevent global conflict, promote international cooperation/peace, and provide a dispute-resolution platform; 70 years after WWII's end (UN's 71st anniversary), member states agreed on the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, an action plan built on "five P's": people, planet, prosperity, peace, and partnerships (the commonly-heard "three P's" — people, planet, prosperity — is a subset); the SDGs replaced the earlier Millennium Development Goals; eradicating poverty in all forms, especially extreme poverty, is identified by the UN as the greatest overarching challenge across all 17 goals.
- **The 17 SDGs (listed in order)** [~20:00]: 1 no poverty, 2 zero hunger, 3 good health and well-being, 4 quality education, 5 gender equality, 6 clean water and sanitation, 7 affordable and clean energy, 8 decent work and economic growth, 9 industry/innovation/infrastructure, 10 reduced inequalities, 11 sustainable cities and communities, 12 responsible consumption and production, 13 climate action, 14 life below water, 15 life on land, 16 peace/justice/strong institutions, 17 partnerships for the goals — framed as an interdependent system (e.g., clean water/energy access underpins other goals; a single planet's sustainability underpins climate/life-below-water/life-on-land goals).
- **SDG targets and tracking** [~20:00]: each SDG has additional sub-targets with measurable indicators; the UN publishes annual progress reports against these indicators; the world is currently off-track on most SDGs — cited as OQI's motivating rationale for applying quantum computing to help accelerate progress, refocusing quantum-computing effort toward benefit for humanity via SDG-targeted challenges/hackathons and use cases.
- **OQI's four-part use-case approach (recap, framed slightly differently from s01/s04)** [~20:00]: (1) multi-stakeholder collaboration — quantum experts, hardware providers, UN/impact experts, and domain experts each take complementary specific tasks; (2) phased development — proposal → simulator implementation → hardware implementation, validated at each phase; (3) community building — exemplified by the training sessions themselves; (4) impact — the explicit focus of this session, embedded in use-case/hackathon design "from the very beginning," not bolted on afterward.
- **Educational resources pointer** [~20:00–40:00]: OQI's website hosts an educational module on use-case-building methodology (QR code given), plus a live Google Forms quiz on session content (SDG five-P's question featured, correct answer "partnership and peace" as the completion of "people, planet, prosperity, ___, ___").
- **"Theory of change" impact-design methodology** [~40:00]: OQI's framework for designing and anticipating a quantum-computing solution's real-world impact, run as a dynamic, continuously-tested/reflected/updated interdisciplinary learning process; works backward from long-term impact (lasting societal change) through midterm impact (behavioral/systemic shifts) and short-term impact (first signals of change, deliverables) down to today's activities (e.g., building a hackathon challenge) and the inputs needed to support them; also requires explicitly identifying assumptions/risks that must hold and the stakeholders involved at each impact horizon.
- **Worked theory-of-change example: fruit/crop production land-allocation use case** [~40:00]: technical task = optimize allocation of a given arable land parcel across crop choices to maximize nutritional value while keeping affordability and minimizing environmental harm (an optimization problem); target SDGs: zero hunger, good health and well-being, responsible consumption and production, climate action. Chain walked through explicitly: activities (building the optimization algorithm) → inputs (a dataset on crop nutrition, sustainability, environmental impact, and affordability, which must be collected) → short-term impact (environmentally-friendly crops actually get grown; requires parallel activities like government-level advocacy conversations, raising awareness of sustainable food production) → mid/long-term impacts (reduced greenhouse gas emissions → reduced global warming; more varied micro/macronutrient diet → sufficient nutritious food → improved school performance in children and improved adult workability → lower healthcare costs; raised awareness → more equitable access, more educated communities, reduced land degradation from agriculture, reduced poverty in farming communities). Assumptions flagged: buyers will purchase what the solution suggests; local supply chains can scale to new crops; farmers will accept farming new crops. Stakeholders by horizon: farmers (short-term), Ministry of Agriculture and buyers/local communities (mid-term), retailers (long-term). Risks by horizon: income inequality / lack of market access (short-term); regulatory barriers, climate change, income inequality again (mid-term); climate change (long-term). Explicit lesson: a quantum-computing solution alone is insufficient — achieving the SDG requires the surrounding system (policy engagement, awareness-raising, supply chains, stakeholder buy-in) to function too.
- **Guided exercise: water-leak-detection use case revisited for impact mapping** [~40:00–60:00]: recaps the technical framing (also covered in Francesca's s04 notes) — cities lose a large share of distribution-network water to leaks (average cited ~39%, higher in some cities); this use case optimizes *placement* of leak-detection sensors (not repair) to find leaks faster; participants are given a document (shared via Discord "live session recording" channel; also available as an alternative through OQI's online "impact tool," which requires account creation) to independently work through the same theory-of-change structure for this use case, with ~5+ minutes of guided self-work time before the solution is walked through live.
- **Water-leak-detection theory-of-change solution walkthrough** [~60:00]: long-term impact = sustainable and equitable access to water; midterm impacts = improved operational response to leak incidents, more efficient water distribution network, reduced water loss — achieved via increased leak identification, increased responsiveness in leak management, increased visibility of water usage; today's activities = mapping the full water distribution network and (if necessary) installing/configuring leak-detection sensors, yielding a digitally-mapped network, known sensor placement, and functional installed water meters. Stakeholders by horizon: quantum computing provider (short-term); utility water company (all levels); UN-Habitat (collaborator across all levels); the city (implied broader level). Risks: unstable digital infrastructure, lacking maintenance, lack of trust, lack of community trust, lack of funding, climate events (short/mid-term); policy or funding shifts, uneven adoption of the solution within the population (long-term) — all resting on underlying assumptions the speaker notes will be shared via document but does not enumerate live in the transcript.
- **Session/module close** [~60:00–80:00]: brief live Q&A window, no substantive new questions recorded; participants directed to ask follow-up questions on Discord (either tagging Audrey directly, or posting in "live session recording"/"homework help"/"general" channels), with Audrey routing module-two-specific questions to the correct original speaker; closes the two-part module-two pre-hackathon training sequence with well-wishes for hackathon selection.
