# Notes — Class 05

## Resumen

Fifth session: a geometric/visual review of single-qubit transformations (rotation, X, Z, Hadamard as reflections), the construction and definition of the Bell state via a Hadamard+CNOT circuit, a long "pet peeve" digression distinguishing quantum entanglement from classical correlation (with an explicit envelope thought experiment), the Toffoli/CCNOT gate, and a fully worked 3-qubit circuit (H, CCNOT, H) demonstrating amplitude cancellation/destructive interference — the session's central "why quantum computing is different" example.

## Conceptos clave

- **Recap of previous session** [~00:00]: quantum state = generalization of classical probability vector (amplitudes replace probabilities, real-restricted for this workshop); unitary matrices = generalization of stochastic matrices (always reversible, unlike stochastic matrices); measurement connects amplitude α_i to probability α_i².
- **Rotation transformation** [~00:00]: R(θ) = [[cos θ, −sin θ],[sin θ, cos θ]], the standard 2D rotation matrix, verified unitary because rotating by −θ inverts it; R(θ)|0⟩ = cos θ|0⟩ + sin θ|1⟩.
- **X (Pauli negation) as reflection** [~00:00]: X = [[0,1],[1,0]], same matrix as the classical NOT/negation transformation (X is a valid transformation both classically and quantum-mechanically); X|0⟩=|1⟩, X|1⟩=|0⟩; by linearity, X(cos θ|0⟩+sin θ|1⟩) = cos θ|1⟩+sin θ|0⟩; geometrically this is a reflection of the unit-circle state across the line y=x.
- **Linearity technique** [~00:00]: to find a transformation's effect on any superposition, first find its effect on the basis states, then combine by linearity — used repeatedly through the session.
- **Z operator as reflection** [~00:00–20:00]: Z leaves |0⟩ unchanged, sends |1⟩ to −|1⟩; not a valid classical transformation (has a negative entry); geometrically a reflection across the x-axis.
- **Hadamard as reflection** [~20:00]: H is a reflection across the axis at angle π/8 (22.5°) relative to the x-axis — this is why H|0⟩ = |+⟩ lands at 45° and H|1⟩ = |−⟩ lands orthogonally in a different quadrant.
- **Limits of single-qubit visualization** [~20:00]: single-qubit transformations have a complete geometric characterization (unit circle / Bloch sphere); no such complete characterization exists yet for 2+ qubit transformations — only partial/subclass understanding.
- **Bell state construction (H then CNOT)** [~20:00]: circuit on |00⟩: apply H to qubit 1 (⊗ identity on qubit 2) → (1/√2)(|0⟩+|1⟩)⊗|0⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩+|10⟩), still separable (no entanglement yet, just superposition on qubit 1); then apply CNOT (qubit 1 = control, qubit 2 = target) → (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩) — this final state is NOT separable, i.e. entangled. This is the canonical Bell state.
- **Bell state = maximally entangled state** [~20:00]: this construction produces one specific example of the broader class of maximally entangled states; the unit of entanglement resource is sometimes called an "ebit" (entangled bit), used to quantify how much entanglement a protocol consumes (details deferred).
- **Bell basis** [~20:00]: alternative basis for 2-qubit states, in addition to the computational basis {|00⟩,|01⟩,|10⟩,|11⟩}: |ψ±⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩±|11⟩), |φ±⟩ = (1/√2)(|01⟩±|10⟩) — four maximally entangled states forming the Bell basis.
- **Working definition of entanglement** [~20:00]: a state is entangled iff it is not separable (cannot be written as a tensor product of individual qubit states) — same definition given in c04, now illustrated concretely.
- **Entanglement detection caveat** [~20:00]: for small numbers of pure qubits (as used here), tests like the PPT (positive partial transpose) test suffice to detect entanglement; no general, precise test exists for arbitrary mixed (noisy) states in general.
- **Pet-peeve digression: popular-science entanglement description is wrong** [~20:00–40:00]: the instructor explicitly refutes the common claim that entanglement enables "instant knowledge" of a distant partner's state as something uniquely quantum — presented via the Alice/Bob-with-Bell-state thought experiment (Alice measures her qubit, instantly "knows" Bob's).
- **Classical envelope thought experiment (counter-example)** [~20:00–40:00]: a referee privately flips a fair coin; heads → both envelopes get red paper, tails → both get green paper; Alice and Bob each take an envelope to their own planet; when Alice opens hers and sees red, she instantly knows Bob's is red too — with zero communication. This reproduces the "instant knowledge" phenomenon entirely classically via perfect correlation created at preparation time, proving that phenomenon alone is not a signature of quantum entanglement.
- **Convention: qubits initialize to |0⟩** [~20:00–40:00]: standard convention (traced to a paper by David DiVincenzo, garbled in transcript as "David Senzo" — likely a mis-transcription of DiVincenzo's criteria for quantum computation) that quantum memory is assumed initialized to |0⟩; not physically required, just a community convention.
- **Subtle real distinction: when is the value determined?** [~20:00–40:00]: classically, the color in the envelope is determined at preparation time (the referee's coin flip), even though Alice/Bob don't know it until they look — a "hidden but fixed" value. In the standard quantum-mechanical interpretation used in this course, the qubit's value is not considered determined until the moment of measurement — quoting Asher Peres: "unperformed experiments have no results." This measurement-time-vs-preparation-time distinction is flagged as subtle, genuinely different between the classical and quantum cases, and practically important in quantum cryptographic protocols (delaying when a value becomes fixed).
- **Non-local correlations (Bell inequality violation)** [~40:00]: the actual uniquely-quantum feature is NOT the "instant knowledge" phenomenon (that's reproducible classically) but the ability of entangled quantum states to generate correlations that cannot be reproduced by classical parties without communication — called non-local correlations, equivalent to violating a Bell inequality. Quantum entanglement includes both classically-reproducible correlations and this uniquely-quantum non-local subset; the deeper exploration of non-local correlations/Bell inequalities is flagged as likely outside the course's scope (focus is on computation).
- **CCNOT / Toffoli gate** [~40:00]: 3-qubit gate, generalizes CNOT with two control bits (x, y) and one target (z); flips the target iff both controls are 1. Valid as both a classical and a quantum transformation (like CNOT) — since it's simultaneously a valid stochastic matrix and a valid unitary matrix, the same gate definition works in either computational model.
- **Worked 3-qubit circuit: H – CCNOT – H** [~40:00–80:00]: initial state |0⟩⊗(α|0⟩+β|1⟩)⊗(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩) (qubit 1 = |0⟩, qubits 2–3 arbitrary superpositions), circuit applies H to qubit 1, then CCNOT on all three, then H again to qubit 1, then measures qubit 1. Naive expectation: since CCNOT doesn't touch qubit 1, the two Hadamards on qubit 1 should cancel (H²=I) and qubit 1 measures 0 with certainty — this expectation turns out to be WRONG.
- **Step-by-step evolution** [~40:00–60:00]: t1 (after first H) = (1/√2)(α|000⟩+β|001⟩+α|100⟩+β|101⟩)⊗... rearranged for CCNOT bookkeeping; t2 (after CCNOT) flips the third qubit exactly when qubits 1&2 are both 1, giving (1/√2)[α|00⟩(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩) + β|01⟩(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩) + α|10⟩(γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩) + β|11⟩(δ|0⟩+γ|1⟩)] (third qubit's γ,δ swapped only in the |11⟩ branch); t3 (after second H on qubit 1) requires fully expanding every term.
- **Amplitude cancellation / destructive interference** [~60:00–80:00]: after expanding t3 and collecting terms by computational basis state, the coefficient of |100⟩ (first qubit=1, others=00) works out to +αγ and −αγ from two different expansion branches, which cancel exactly to zero — meaning the joint state |100⟩ never occurs, with zero probability, even though each contributing branch individually had a nonzero amplitude. This cancellation is impossible classically (classical probabilities are non-negative and can only add constructively) — explicitly identified as the crux of the session's teaching point: signed (or complex) amplitudes permit constructive AND destructive interference, which is the mechanism giving quantum computation its distinctive power.
- **Final collected state (t3)**, with a factor of 1/2 out front: contains terms |000⟩ (coeff 2αγ, i.e. combined so overall coefficient αγ after normalization bookkeeping — see transcript for exact collection), |001⟩ (2αδ), |010⟩ and |011⟩ (β(γ+δ)), |110⟩ (β(γ−δ)), |111⟩ (β(δ−γ)) — notably |100⟩ and |101⟩ do NOT appear (cancelled to zero). [Exact normalized coefficients were derived live and corrected mid-lecture after a student caught a missing cancellation — treat the coefficients as illustrative of the structure rather than a guaranteed-typo-free final expression; see Huecos.]
- **Probability qubit 1 measures 1** [~60:00–80:00]: computed by summing the squared amplitudes of all surviving terms with first qubit = 1 (i.e. the |110⟩ and |111⟩ terms), giving P(qubit1=1) = (β²/2)(γ−δ)² — a striking result because a naive analysis (ignoring interference) would have predicted qubit 1 always measures 0.
- **Conditional probability of qubit 2 given qubit 1 = 1** [~80:00]: collecting only the surviving |11x⟩ terms shows qubit 2 is found in state 1 with certainty (probability 1) — a direct consequence of the same cancellation that removed the |10x⟩ terms.
- **Choosing parameters to force P(qubit1=1)=1** [~80:00]: shown to be achievable, e.g. α=0, β=1, δ=1/√2, γ=−1/√2 satisfies (β²/2)(γ−δ)²=1 — demonstrating the interference effect can be tuned to certainty, not just partial probability.
- **Closing framing** [~80:00]: the whole exercise's purpose was to demonstrate that superposition + entanglement created inside a circuit can produce measurement outcomes that would be impossible/unexpected under naive classical-style reasoning about the circuit — this amplitude-cancellation mechanism is the version of "superpower" the course has been building toward, and is the segue into quantum algorithms in subsequent sessions. ## Fórmulas y definiciones

- **Rotation matrix**: R(θ) = [[cos θ, −sin θ], [sin θ, cos θ]]; unitary since R(θ)⁻¹ = R(−θ). [~00:00]
- **Pauli-X**: X = [[0,1],[1,0]]; X|0⟩=|1⟩, X|1⟩=|0⟩ — valid classical AND quantum transformation. [~00:00]
- **Pauli-Z**: Z|0⟩=|0⟩, Z|1⟩=−|1⟩ — not a valid classical transformation (negative entry). [~00:00–20:00]
- **Hadamard as reflection**: reflection axis at angle π/8 relative to the x-axis. [~20:00]
- **Bell state (this construction)**: (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩), produced by CNOT·(H⊗I)|00⟩. [~20:00]
- **Bell basis**: |ψ±⟩=(1/√2)(|00⟩±|11⟩), |φ±⟩=(1/√2)(|01⟩±|10⟩). [~20:00]
- **CCNOT / Toffoli gate**: 3-qubit gate on (x,y,z); flips z iff x=y=1, else identity — valid classical and quantum transformation. [~40:00]
- **P(qubit 1 = 1) in the worked circuit**: (β²/2)(γ−δ)² — derived from collecting the surviving interference terms of the H–CCNOT–H circuit. [~60:00–80:00]
- **P(qubit 2 = 1 | qubit 1 = 1) = 1** in the same circuit — certainty, from the surviving |11x⟩ terms only. [~80:00]

## Glosario

- **Ebit (entangled bit)**: unit of resource for quantifying entanglement in a maximally entangled state / protocol. [~20:00]
- **Bell state**: the maximally entangled 2-qubit state (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩) (or its ± variants). [~20:00]
- **Bell basis**: the four maximally entangled 2-qubit states {ψ±, φ±} forming an alternative basis to the computational basis. [~20:00]
- **PPT test (positive partial transpose)**: entanglement-detection test sufficient for small numbers of pure qubits. [~20:00]
- **Non-local correlation / Bell inequality violation**: the subset of quantum correlations that cannot be reproduced by classical parties without communication — the genuinely unique feature of quantum entanglement. [~40:00]
- **Toffoli gate (CCNOT)**: doubly-controlled NOT gate; central to reversible computation theory. [~40:00]
- **Constructive/destructive interference (amplitude cancellation)**: the phenomenon whereby signed amplitudes from different computational paths add or cancel, unlike non-negative classical probabilities which can only add. [~60:00–80:00]
- **Asher Peres's dictum**: "unperformed experiments have no results" — used to justify treating a qubit's value as undetermined until measured, contrasted with the classical case where the value is fixed at preparation. [~20:00–40:00]
