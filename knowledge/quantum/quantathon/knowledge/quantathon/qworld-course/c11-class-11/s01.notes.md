# Notes — Class 11

## Resumen

Final session of the QWorld course. Starts from classical simulated annealing as motivation, introduces the quantum adiabatic theorem and adiabatic quantum computing, works through the linear-algebra prerequisite of exponentiating Hermitian matrices (diagonalization, Euler-identity-style formula for Pauli matrices), uses Trotterization to justify discretizing adiabatic evolution into alternating mixer/cost unitaries, and arrives at the full QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm) construction: mixer Hamiltonian, cost Hamiltonian from the Ising/QUBO correspondence, the parameterized circuit ψ(γ,β), and the classical outer-loop optimization of γ,β. Closes with logistics for the Canvas assessments (BQM + quantum annealing on D-Wave), the hackathon's second workshop module, and general advice on reducing real-world problems to known CS problems to identify applicable (quantum) algorithms.

## Conceptos clave

- **Simulated annealing (classical)** [~00:00]: metallurgy-inspired optimization heuristic — heat up then slowly cool a system so it settles into a low-energy/uniform crystal configuration; framed as an optimization algorithm analogous to Newton-Raphson or gradient descent for finding minima/maxima on a "terrain."
- **Neighborhood search + acceptance rule** [~00:00]: from a current solution S, pick a neighboring candidate S′; if S′ is better, always move; if worse, still move with some non-zero probability (controlled by an annealing/cooling schedule, not detailed) — this allows escaping local optima, unlike pure greedy descent which gets stuck in local minima vs. the desired global minimum.
- **Simulated annealing as a heuristic, not a domain-specific algorithm** [~00:00]: it is heuristic because it isn't tailored to the structure of a specific problem, just perturbs and stochastically accepts worse moves; contrasted with the quantum adiabatic algorithm, which does derive from an actual physical process.
- **Adiabatic quantum computing (AQC) origin** [~00:00]: proposed by Farhi et al. c. 2003–2004 as an alternative model of quantum computation; later shown equivalent to the standard circuit model (proof not covered) — the course only motivates where quantum annealing (extracted from AQC ideas) comes from, as an NISQ-era approximate algorithm.
- **Quantum adiabatic theorem** [~00:00]: if a system starts in the ground state of an easy-to-prepare initial Hamiltonian H_i and evolves according to the Schrödinger equation slowly enough toward a final (cost) Hamiltonian H_f, the system remains in the instantaneous ground state throughout, ending in the ground state of H_f at t=1 — measuring then yields the ground-state energy, i.e. the solution to the encoded problem.
- **"Slow enough" condition** [~00:00]: governed by the spectral gap between the ground state and the next-largest eigenvalue during the evolution; if this gap becomes too small, there is non-negligible probability of the system jumping to a higher energy level, breaking the guarantee. Controlling this gap for an arbitrary problem Hamiltonian is itself a hard problem — flagged explicitly as a technical limitation, not resolved.
- **Evolution path is not necessarily linear** [~00:00]: H(t) need not interpolate linearly from H_i to H_f; other paths are possible but raise the same open question of guaranteeing a large-enough spectral gap throughout.
- **Matrix exponential prerequisite — diagonalizable (Hermitian) case** [~00:00–20:00]: for Hermitian H = UDU†, H^n = U D^n U†, hence e^H = Σ H^n/n! = U e^D U†, and since D is diagonal, e^D is just the entrywise exponential of the diagonal — this makes matrix exponentials of Hermitian operators tractable.
- **Worked example: e^(−iθP) for a Pauli matrix P (P²=I)** [~00:00–20:00]: because P²=I, the exponential collapses to cos(θ)I − i sin(θ)P (an Euler-identity analogue for involutory matrices); instantiated for the X Pauli, showing the result is the standard rotation matrix R_x (up to the factor of 2 in the angle convention) — illustrating the link between continuous Hamiltonian evolution and discrete gate application.
- **Quantum annealing vs. simulated annealing** [~20:00]: where simulated annealing must thermally climb over a barrier in the energy landscape, quantum annealing exploits quantum tunneling, giving non-negligible probability of the state passing through the barrier directly rather than over it — this tunneling is the resource QAOA tries to harness on NISQ devices.
- **Discretizing the adiabatic unitary** [~20:00]: the continuous evolution operator U(0,T) is approximated as a product of small-time-step unitaries U(t_k, t_{k-1}), with t_k = kΔt; each small step approximated as e^{−iH(t_k)Δt}.
- **Time-dependent Hamiltonian H(t) in the adiabatic schedule** [~20:00]: H(t) = (1 − t/T)H_i + (t/T)H_f, so the discretized unitary product becomes Π_k e^{−i[(1 − kΔt/T)H_i + (kΔt/T)H_f]Δt}.
- **Trotterization** [~20:00]: since H_i and H_f generally do not commute (H_i H_f ≠ H_f H_i), e^{−i(H_1+H_2)} ≠ e^{−iH_1}e^{−iH_2} in general; the Trotter formula gives e^{−i(H_1+H_2)Δt} = e^{−iH_1Δt}e^{−iH_2Δt} + O(Δt²) — used to split each discretized step into separate H_i and H_f exponentials.
- **QAOA circuit structure emerges from Trotterized adiabatic evolution** [~20:00]: iteratively applying alternating unitaries built from H_i (the "mixer") and H_f (the cost Hamiltonian) approximates true adiabatic evolution better as the number of repetitions (layers) increases — this iterative alternating structure *is* the QAOA ansatz.
- **QAOA initial state and mixer Hamiltonian H_B** [~20:00]: initial state chosen as |S⟩ = H^⊗n|0⟩^n (the n-fold |+⟩ state), which is easy to prepare and must be the ground state of the mixer Hamiltonian H_B; H_B = −Σᵢ Xᵢ (sum of Pauli-X on each qubit, negated so |+⟩^⊗n is the eigenstate with eigenvalue −1, i.e. the ground state); called the "mixer" because applying X flips bits, acting as the source of quantum fluctuations.
- **Mixer unitary U(H_B, β_p)** [~20:00–40:00]: from the Pauli matrix-exponential identity above, U(H_B,β_p) = Π_i R_x(2β_p) applied to each qubit i (single-qubit rotations around X).
- **Cost Hamiltonian H_C** [~20:00–40:00]: reuses the QUBO/Ising-derived form H_C = Σᵢ hᵢZᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Jᵢⱼ ZᵢZⱼ (linked explicitly back to the Ising model from the previous session, c10); this is the Hamiltonian whose ground state encodes the optimization problem's solution.
- **Cost unitary U(H_C, γ_p)** [~20:00–40:00]: U(H_C,γ_p) = e^{−iγ_p H_C}; the linear (Zᵢ) terms give single-qubit Z-rotations R_z(2h_iγ_p), and the quadratic (ZᵢZⱼ) terms give two-qubit ZZ-rotations R_zz(2J_ijγ_p); R_zz(θ) = e^{−i(θ/2) Z⊗Z}, a diagonal matrix with entries e^{±iθ/2} (all diagonal entries e^{iθ/2} except two carrying a minus sign).
- **Full QAOA state (p layers)** [~40:00]: |ψ(γ,β)⟩ = U(H_B,β_p) U(H_C,γ_p) ⋯ U(H_B,β_1) U(H_C,γ_1) |S⟩, matching the alternating-exponential form derived from the Trotterized adiabatic approximation.
- **Classical optimization outer loop** [~40:00]: starting from initial parameters (γ₀,β₀), prepare |ψ(γ₀,β₀)⟩, measure the expectation value ⟨ψ|H_C|ψ⟩, then classically update the parameter list to (γ₁,β₁) and repeat, iteratively driving the expectation value down toward the ground-state energy of H_C — explicitly connected back to both the variational principle (from c10's VQA/VQE discussion) and the adiabatic/quantum-annealing motivation from earlier in this session.
- **γ and β are parameter *lists*, not scalars** [~40:00]: clarified in response to a student question — for p layers there are p independent γ values (γ_1,...,γ_p) and p independent β values (β_1,...,β_p), one pair per layer, not a single repeated scalar.
- **Limit p→∞ recovers continuous adiabatic evolution** [~40:00–60:00]: increasing the number of layers p corresponds to finer discretization (smaller Δt) of the original continuous unitary; in the limit, QAOA approaches true (continuous) adiabatic evolution.
- **QAOA applied to MaxCut** [~40:00–60:00]: take the Ising Hamiltonian already derived for MaxCut (session c10) and use it directly as H_C in the QAOA construction above — the rest of the algorithm is then fully specified and just needs to be run.
- **Problem-reduction advice (closing remarks)** [~40:00–60:00]: general algorithmic problem-solving guidance — abstract away real-world problem details first and identify which known computer-science problem the real problem reduces to; this identifies the applicable class of algorithms "for free." Only afterward return to the original problem's specific structure/constraints, since exploiting special structure (e.g., the bipartite-graph special case for MaxCut, session c08) can reduce complexity below the general NP-hard case — described as searching for a "Goldilocks" subclass that remains classically hard but becomes tractable via a quantum or hybrid approach.
- **Course/assessment logistics (closing)** [~40:00–60:00]: this session's Canvas module has fewer assessments than earlier sessions — two on Binary Quadratic Models (QUBO via D-Wave's library) and two on running quantum annealing on D-Wave's platform; running on actual D-Wave hardware is explicitly not required (only using their library), since not everyone has hardware access. A second hackathon-related workshop module (led by Audrey from OQI, separate meeting link posted on Discord) covers hackathon expectations/judging feedback, not additional graded assessments. ## Fórmulas y definiciones

- **Hermitian matrix exponential**: for H = UDU† (D diagonal with H's eigenvalues), H^n = UD^nU†, so e^H = U e^D U†, with e^D computed entrywise on the diagonal. [~00:00–20:00]
- **Pauli-matrix exponential identity**: for P with P²=I, e^{−iθP} = cos(θ)I − i sin(θ)P. [~00:00–20:00]
- **R_x from e^{−iθX}**: e^{−iθX} = cos(θ)I − i sin(θ)X, i.e. the standard X-rotation matrix (up to angle-convention factor of 2). [~00:00–20:00]
- **Discretized adiabatic unitary**: U(T,0) ≈ Π_{k=0}^{n} e^{−iH(t_k)Δt}, with t_k = kΔt. [~20:00]
- **Adiabatic schedule Hamiltonian**: H(t) = (1 − t/T)H_i + (t/T)H_f. [~20:00]
- **Trotter formula (first order)**: e^{−i(H_1+H_2)Δt} = e^{−iH_1Δt} e^{−iH_2Δt} + O(Δt²), valid even when H_1,H_2 don't commute. [~20:00]
- **Trotterized adiabatic step**: e^{−i[(1−kΔt/T)H_i + (kΔt/T)H_f]Δt} ≈ e^{−i(1−kΔt/T)H_iΔt} · e^{−i(kΔt/T)H_fΔt}. [~20:00]
- **Mixer Hamiltonian**: H_B = −Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ; ground state |+⟩^⊗n with eigenvalue −1. [~20:00]
- **Mixer unitary**: U(H_B, β_p) = Πᵢ R_x,i(2β_p). [~20:00–40:00]
- **Cost Hamiltonian (QUBO/Ising form, from c10)**: H_C = Σᵢ hᵢZᵢ + Σᵢⱼ JᵢⱼZᵢZⱼ. [~20:00–40:00]
- **Cost unitary**: U(H_C, γ_p) = e^{−iγ_p H_C} = Πᵢ R_z,i(2hᵢγ_p) · Πᵢⱼ R_zz,ij(2Jᵢⱼγ_p). [~20:00–40:00]
- **R_zz rotation**: R_zz(θ) = e^{−i(θ/2) Z⊗Z}, diagonal with entries e^{iθ/2} except two entries carrying a minus sign in the exponent (e^{−iθ/2}). [~20:00–40:00]
- **QAOA state (p layers)**: |ψ(γ,β)⟩ = [Π_{p'=1}^{p} U(H_B,β_{p'}) U(H_C,γ_{p'})] |S⟩, |S⟩ = H^⊗n|0⟩^n. [~40:00]
- **QAOA objective / measured quantity**: ⟨ψ(γ,β)|H_C|ψ(γ,β)⟩, minimized over (γ,β) via classical outer-loop optimization to approach the ground-state energy of H_C. [~40:00]

## Glosario

- **Simulated annealing**: classical heuristic optimization algorithm inspired by metallurgical annealing; perturbs a current solution and probabilistically accepts worse moves to escape local optima. [~00:00]
- **Adiabatic quantum computing (AQC)**: model of quantum computation based on slowly evolving a quantum system's Hamiltonian from an easy initial one to a problem-encoding final one; proven equivalent to the circuit model. [~00:00]
- **Quantum adiabatic theorem**: guarantees that a system evolved slowly enough (relative to its spectral gap) remains in its instantaneous ground state, ending in the ground state of the final Hamiltonian. [~00:00]
- **Spectral gap**: the energy difference between the ground state and the next-highest eigenvalue; must stay sufficiently large throughout adiabatic evolution for the theorem's guarantee to hold. [~00:00]
- **Quantum tunneling (in quantum annealing)**: mechanism allowing a quantum state to pass through an energy barrier rather than over it, in contrast to simulated annealing's thermal climb. [~20:00]
- **Trotterization / Trotter formula**: approximation splitting the exponential of a sum of non-commuting Hamiltonians into a product of individual exponentials, with an O(Δt²) error term. [~20:00]
- **QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm)**: hybrid quantum-classical algorithm derived from Trotterized adiabatic evolution, alternating mixer and cost unitaries with classically-optimized parameters (γ,β). [~20:00]
- **Mixer Hamiltonian (H_B)**: the "initial" Hamiltonian in the QAOA/adiabatic analogy, here −ΣXᵢ, whose ground state is the easy-to-prepare |+⟩^⊗n state; source of quantum fluctuations/mixing. [~20:00]
- **Cost Hamiltonian (H_C)**: the "final" Hamiltonian encoding the optimization problem, built from the QUBO/Ising form derived in c10. [~20:00–40:00]
- **Layers / repetitions (p)**: the number of alternating mixer/cost unitary blocks in the QAOA circuit; more layers approximate continuous adiabatic evolution more closely. [~40:00–60:00]
- **Problem reduction**: the algorithmic-thinking step of abstracting a real-world problem into a known computer-science problem so its established algorithms/complexity results apply. [~40:00–60:00]
