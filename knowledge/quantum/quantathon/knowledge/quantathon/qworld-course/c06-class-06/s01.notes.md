# Notes — Class 06

## Resumen

Sixth session: a hands-on Qiskit introduction (registers, gates, simulation, shots) used to verify the previous session's H–CCNOT–H interference circuit, followed by the two "pioneering" quantum protocols — superdense coding and quantum teleportation — worked through in full circuit detail, with an extended discussion of what these protocols do and don't demonstrate (contrasted against popular-science/sci-fi framings of teleportation).

## Conceptos clave

- **Qiskit basics** [~00:00]: QuantumRegister, ClassicalRegister, QuantumCircuit are the three core imports; quantum registers hold qubits, classical registers hold classical bits used to store measurement outcomes; qubits indexed from 0, top-to-bottom by default in circuit diagrams (order reversible for display, but arbitrary/conventional — like little/big-endian, just pick one and be consistent).
- **Circuit construction and drawing** [~00:00]: gates applied via `qc.x(q0)`, `qc.h(q0)`, etc.; measurement writes a qubit's result into a specified classical bit index; barriers are purely visual/logical partitions with no computational effect; double parallel lines in circuit diagrams denote classical-information flow, single lines denote quantum-information flow.
- **Simulation and shots** [~00:00]: circuits are run via a simulator (e.g. Aer) for a specified number of shots (e.g. 1024) because quantum outputs are probabilistic — a single run only samples one outcome, so many shots are needed to estimate the underlying probability distribution (directly analogous to needing many classical coin flips to estimate its bias). All circuits run in this course are classical simulations of quantum circuits, not execution on real quantum hardware (an optional exercise near the end of the workshop covers that).
- **Verification of the previous day's H–CCNOT–H circuit** [~00:00–20:00]: rebuilt in Qiskit with extra preparation gates (X, X-then-H) to set qubit 2 to |1⟩ and qubit 3 to |−⟩ — the exact parameter choice from c05 shown to force P(qubit1=1)=1; running the simulator confirms measurement result 1 with certainty across all shots, verifying the hand computation from the prior session.
- **Superdense coding setup** [~00:00–20:00]: Alice and Bob pre-share a Bell state |φ+⟩=(1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩), physically separated (first qubit of each ket-term is Alice's share, second is Bob's — a common point of confusion flagged explicitly); Alice additionally holds two classical bits a, b that she wants to communicate to Bob.
- **Superdense coding circuit** [~00:00–20:00]: Alice applies a classically-controlled Z gate on her qubit if a=1, then a classically-controlled X (CNOT, with b as a classical control) if b=1; she then physically transmits her (now-transformed) qubit to Bob over a quantum channel; Bob, now holding both qubits, applies CNOT (qubit1=control, qubit2=target) then Hadamard on qubit 1, and measures both qubits to recover exactly a and b.
- **Key claim: 1 quantum bit + 1 pre-shared ebit ⟹ transmits 2 classical bits** [~00:00–20:00]: this is why the protocol is called superdense coding — compressing 2 classical bits of information transmission into 1 physical qubit transmission, using shared entanglement as a resource. Explicitly framed as a proof-of-concept about quantum information's compressive power, not a practically useful communication scheme (no claimed practical advantage given the technological overhead of maintaining/transmitting entanglement for only a 2x factor).
- **Exhaustive 4-case verification (a,b ∈ {00,01,10,11})** [~20:00–40:00]: Alice's two controlled operations transform the shared |φ+⟩ into one of the four Bell basis states depending on (a,b): (0,0)→|φ+⟩, (0,1)→|ψ+⟩, (1,0)→|φ−⟩, (1,1)→|ψ−⟩ (up to a global phase in one case, discussed below). Bob's CNOT+H sequence is exactly the inverse of the Bell-state preparation circuit (H then CNOT), so applying it to each Bell state maps it back to the corresponding computational basis state |a,b⟩ — i.e. Bob is converting from the Bell basis to the computational basis.
- **Entanglement "disappears" via basis change, not measurement** [~20:00–40:00]: a student's question ("where did the entanglement go?") is answered by noting Bob's CNOT operation disconnects the correlation between the two qubits (e.g. rewriting so the second qubit becomes deterministically fixed while the first carries the |+⟩/|−⟩ information) — described as a destructive-interference-like cancellation of the |10⟩ term, not a probabilistic effect, but structurally similar to the interference seen in c05.
- **Global phase note** [~20:00–40:00]: in the (a,b)=(1,1) case, Bob's final state comes out as −|11⟩ rather than |11⟩; this leading minus sign is an unobservable global phase — squaring it in the Born rule removes any physical/experimental distinction from |11⟩, so it does not indicate an error in the protocol.
- **Alternative: Bob could measure directly in the Bell basis** [~20:00–40:00]: rather than transforming back to the computational basis, Bob could instead perform a joint Bell-basis measurement on both qubits immediately upon receiving Alice's qubit — this would still recover (a,b) via the mapping (φ+→00, ψ+→01, φ−→10, ψ−→11) without needing the extra CNOT+H "un-preparation" step, though generalized/joint measurements are noted as outside the course's main scope.
- **Quantum teleportation setup** [~40:00–60:00]: historical aside — the original teleportation paper was initially rejected, then championed by reviewer David Mermin (name likely intended; recommended as an author whose expositions of quantum foundations are worth reading) before publication (~early 1990s); IBM later ran a Scientific American ad about "teleporting" goulash, which the instructor treats skeptically. Circuit structure is roughly the reverse of superdense coding: Alice starts with one unknown qubit |ψ⟩=α|0⟩+β|1⟩ (she need not know α, β) plus her share of a pre-shared |φ+⟩ with Bob; she applies the Bell-state-preparation-inverse sequence (CNOT then Hadamard) jointly on her qubit and her share of |φ+⟩, then measures both of her qubits, obtaining two classical bits; she sends these two classical bits (over a classical channel) to Bob, who applies a conditional X and/or Z (in the order X-then-Z) to his remaining qubit to recover |ψ⟩ exactly.
- **Contrast with sci-fi teleportation** [~40:00–60:00]: this protocol is explicitly NOT instantaneous (Bob cannot apply his correction until he receives Alice's classical bits, so there's an unavoidable communication-time/light-speed-bounded delay) and does NOT violate the no-cloning theorem (Alice's original qubit is destroyed by her measurement, not copied) — no new matter is created; the framing "information is physical" is invoked to argue the resulting qubit is genuinely indistinguishable from the original, not merely "like" it.
- **Why teleportation is nontrivial despite this** [~40:00–60:00]: (1) an arbitrary, unknown quantum state (with amplitudes of arbitrary precision) is exactly reproduced at Bob's end using only 2 classical bits + prior shared entanglement — a stronger result than superdense coding's classical-bit transmission; (2) if Alice's original |ψ⟩ was itself entangled with a third party Charlie (never touched by the protocol), the teleported qubit at Bob's end remains entangled with Charlie in exactly the same way — entanglement structure with third parties is preserved through teleportation.
- **Open/philosophical aside (explicitly flagged as outside course scope)** [~40:00–60:00]: during the interval after Alice measures but before Bob applies his correction, the state |ψ⟩ is described as "smeared" across Alice's classical bits and Bob's uncorrected qubit — not fully residing anywhere; the instructor explicitly declines to go into deeper philosophical/foundational territory but flags it as a genuinely interesting open scientific question, distinct from "spooky action at a distance" (this protocol is not claimed to be that).
- **Full worked calculation of teleportation** [~60:00–80:00]: starting state |ψ⟩⊗|φ+⟩ expanded, CNOT applied (control=qubit1/ψ's, target=qubit2/Alice's share of φ+), then Hadamard on qubit 1 — full expansion yields four branches grouped by Alice's two-qubit measurement outcome: (00)→α|0⟩+β|1⟩=|ψ⟩ exactly (no correction needed); (01)→α|1⟩+β|0⟩, requiring Bob to apply X to recover |ψ⟩; (10)→α|0⟩−β|1⟩, requiring Bob to apply Z; (11)→α|1⟩−β|0⟩, requiring Bob to apply X then Z (order matters).
- **Connection to quantum error correction** [~60:00–80:00]: the instructor explicitly draws the analogy that Alice's two classical bits function exactly like an error syndrome in QEC — encoding which of the two "fundamental" qubit errors (bit-flip X, phase-flip Z) has effectively occurred relative to |ψ⟩, telling Bob which correction(s) to apply; since X and Z errors form a basis for all single-qubit errors, this foreshadows general error-correction structure (not developed further here).
- **Generalization note** [~60:00–80:00]: teleporting a larger/higher-dimensional state is possible but requires proportionally more shared entanglement and more classical communication — the resource cost scales with the state being teleported, not free.
- **Roadmap** [~60:00–80:00]: next session will generalize the two/three-qubit toy-model mathematics to n qubits and introduce a non-trivial quantum algorithm.

## Fórmulas y definiciones

- **Shared Bell state for both protocols**: |φ+⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩), qubit 1 = Alice's share, qubit 2 = Bob's share. [~00:00]
- **Superdense coding encoding map**: (a,b)=(0,0)→|φ+⟩, (0,1)→|ψ+⟩, (1,0)→|φ−⟩, (1,1)→|ψ−⟩ (up to global phase), via classically-controlled Z (if a=1) then classically-controlled X (if b=1) on Alice's qubit. [~00:00–40:00]
- **Superdense coding decoding**: Bob applies CNOT (qubit1 control, qubit2 target) then H (qubit1), mapping the Bell basis back to the computational basis {|00⟩,|01⟩,|10⟩,|11⟩} = (a,b). [~20:00–40:00]
- **Teleportation circuit**: Alice applies CNOT(ψ→φ+'s share) then H(ψ); measures both her qubits → (m1,m2); sends (m1,m2) classically to Bob; Bob applies Xᵐ² then Zᵐ¹ (correction order: X first, then Z) to recover |ψ⟩=α|0⟩+β|1⟩ exactly. [~60:00–80:00]
- **Four teleportation branches**: (00)→|ψ⟩ (no correction); (01)→X⁻¹ needed (α|1⟩+β|0⟩); (10)→Z⁻¹ needed (α|0⟩−β|1⟩); (11)→X then Z needed (α|1⟩−β|0⟩). [~60:00–80:00]

## Glosario

- **Shot (Qiskit)**: one repeated execution of a quantum circuit on a simulator/hardware, needed in bulk to estimate a probability distribution over outcomes. [~00:00]
- **Barrier**: a purely visual/logical partition marker in a circuit diagram, with no computational effect. [~00:00]
- **Superdense coding**: protocol transmitting 2 classical bits via 1 quantum bit transmission plus 1 pre-shared entangled (Bell) pair. [~00:00–40:00]
- **Quantum teleportation**: protocol transmitting an unknown qubit state exactly via 2 classical bits plus 1 pre-shared entangled (Bell) pair, destroying the original in the process (consistent with no-cloning). [~40:00–80:00]
- **No-cloning theorem**: it is impossible to create an identical copy of an arbitrary unknown quantum state — referenced to explain why teleportation doesn't produce a duplicate, only a relocated original. [~40:00–60:00]
- **Global phase**: an overall unobservable phase factor (e.g. −1) on a quantum state, undetectable by any measurement since |−ψ⟩ and |ψ⟩ give identical Born-rule probabilities. [~20:00–40:00]
- **Error syndrome (QEC analogy)**: classical information (here, Alice's two measured bits) that identifies which correction operation(s) must be applied — directly analogous to how superdense-coding/teleportation classical bits function in this session. [~60:00–80:00]
