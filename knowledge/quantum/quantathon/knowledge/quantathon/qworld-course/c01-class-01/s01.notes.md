# Notes — Class 01 (Welcome Session)

## Resumen

Welcome/logistics session for the QWorld × OQI pre-hackathon training program (10 QWorld sessions + a separate 5-session OQI module). Covers how to enroll (Canvas, Discord, GitLab), the certification structure (Q-Bronze / Q-Nickel-partial / Q-Cobalt), assessment rules, and hackathon-specific deadlines. No quantum-computing technical content in this session — that starts in c02.

## Conceptos clave

- **Program structure** [~00:00]: 10 days of QWorld sessions (Q-Bronze, a partial Q-Nickel, Q-Cobalt) starting May 15, plus a second, separate 5-session module organized by OQI (May 27–29, June 4–5) focused on applying quantum computing to a hackathon problem — ungraded, attendance-only.
- **Certification** [~00:00]: two certificates available — Q-Bronze (introductory) and Q-Cobalt (more directly relevant to hackathon-style work). Criteria: score ≥50% on each individual assessment AND ≥70% overall across a module's assessments.
- **Platforms** [~00:00]: Canvas (LMS, hosts assessments), Discord (all communication, lecture links, recorded video links, mentor support), GitLab (combined Bronze+Nickel+Cobalt Jupyter notebooks, auto-installs dependencies), Zoom (live sessions, same link reused).
- **Hackathon deadlines** [~00:00]: Morocco/Mexico/Vietnam → May 30, Anywhere on Earth (AoE); Costa Rica → June 6, AoE. Assessments remain accessible on Canvas past the deadline (platform limitation), but it's the participant's responsibility to submit on time for their hackathon.
- **Assessment types** [~00:00]: simple quizzes (MCQ / fill-in-the-blank, 3 attempts each) early on; programming-notebook assignments (auto-graded, 10 attempts) later in the program.
- **Prerequisites** [~20:00]: basic linear algebra and some programming experience; no computer science degree required — high schoolers have completed Q-Bronze successfully before.
- **Q-Cobalt / NISQ framing** [~20:00]: the instructor distinguishes decades-old theoretical quantum computing results from the current NISQ-era practice, where algorithms are evaluated more heuristically ("can I get this to work better?") rather than via provable guarantees. The latter half of the workshop leans heavily on optimization problems and their quantum algorithm applications.
